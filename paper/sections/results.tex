Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:methods} show that distillation helps in some pairs but not in others.
\Kd beats \Scratch on TrashNet for both MobileNets, by $+0.025\pm0.008$ (MobileNetV2-0.35) and $+0.009\pm0.007$ (MobileNetV3-Small) macro-F1, and it is ahead on all three seeds of each. On LeNet-5 it hurts ($-0.045\pm0.093$), while \Rkd helps ($+0.049\pm0.035$).
The best mean is \Kd on the TrashNet MobileNets, \Rkd on TrashNet/LeNet-5 and RPS-25/MobileNetV2-0.35, \Rbf on RPS-25/MobileNetV3-Small, and \Scratch on RPS-25/LeNet-5. \Pqk is best on no pair.
\Rbf beats \Scratch on the mean in only \RbfBeatsScratch{} of \NPairs{} pairs. Its largest gain is on RPS-25/MobileNetV3-Small ($+0.072\pm0.034$), where it is ahead on all three seeds.
On TrashNet all students stay well below the teacher (best $0.732$ against $0.876$ macro-F1). On RPS-25 the best student ($0.888$) comes close to the teacher's $0.907$.
Seed noise is large relative to these effects. The standard deviation across seeds ranges from $0.012$ to $0.202$ on RPS-25 and from $0.003$ to $0.074$ on TrashNet.

\subsection{PQK against logit KD, the RBF twin and relational KD}
\label{sec:results-pqk}

\paragraph{Against logit KD}
\Pqk and \Kd are not distinguishable (Table~\ref{tab:pqk-kd}).
At fp32, \Pqk is ahead on the mean in \KdAheadFp{} of \NPairs{} pairs, or in \KdAheadFpEx{} if the one \Kd seed trained at a different temperature is excluded.
On TrashNet, the primary dataset, it is behind on both MobileNets, by $0.025\pm0.025$ and $0.014\pm0.021$ macro-F1, and ahead on one seed of three in each.
It is ahead on TrashNet/LeNet-5 ($+0.051\pm0.073$), where \Kd itself fails. On RPS-25 the differences are $+0.003$, $+0.010$ and $-0.013$ on macro-F1, and $+0.012$, $+0.008$ and $-0.010$ on top-1, the primary metric there.
After INT8 conversion, \Pqk is ahead in \KdAheadPc{} of \NExported{} pairs on PC numerics and on the Pi's builtin kernels, and in \KdAheadXnn{} of \NExported{} with the Pi's default XNNPACK kernels.
TrashNet/MobileNetV3-Small is a loss under all three ($-0.017$, $-0.016$, $-0.025$), with \Pqk behind on every seed. The RPS-25 differences ($+0.011$ to $+0.032$) come with paired standard deviations of $0.10$ to $0.12$.
No difference is significant; the smallest uncorrected $p$-value is \MinPKd{} at fp32 and \MinPKdInt{} at INT8.
The comparison is not matched on temperature, because the \Kd temperature was screened while the kernel methods use one untuned $\tau$.
Against \Scratch, \Pqk differs by $-0.039$ to $+0.024$ across the six pairs (smallest $p=\MinPScratch{}$).

\paragraph{Against the RBF twin}
At the pre-specified $\lambda=1$, \Pqk is ahead of its twin on the mean in only \GtwoFixedWins{} of \NPairs{} pairs (Table~\ref{tab:g2}). These are TrashNet/MobileNetV2-0.35 ($+0.023\pm0.016$, three of three seeds) and RPS-25/LeNet-5 ($+0.100\pm0.145$, two of three). It is behind on the other four ($-0.042$, $-0.004$, $-0.028$, $-0.048$).
The two winning pairs were the first to be evaluated, and the other four did not reproduce their result.
The adaptive $\lambda$ gives \Pqk the same calibration as the twin. With it, \Pqk is ahead on \GtwoAdaptWins{} of \GtwoAdaptPairs{} pairs ($+0.013\pm0.011$, $+0.003\pm0.036$, $+0.029\pm0.088$) and behind on RPS-25/MobileNetV3-Small ($-0.058\pm0.011$), winning on $3$, $2$, $1$ and $0$ seeds respectively.
The smallest $p$-value is \MinPRbfFixed{} for $\lambda=1$. For the adaptive variant it is \MinPRbfAdapt{}, and that difference favours \MinPRbfAdaptFavours.
The quantum kernel therefore shows no consistent advantage over its classical counterpart.

\paragraph{Against relational KD}
\Rkd is the classical counterpart of QRKD's quantum extension.
\Pqk is ahead of \Rkd on the two TrashNet MobileNet pairs ($+0.012\pm0.016$ and $+0.003\pm0.008$). It is behind on TrashNet/LeNet-5 ($-0.043\pm0.018$, on every seed, $p=0.052$), RPS-25/MobileNetV2-0.35 ($-0.024\pm0.023$) and RPS-25/MobileNetV3-Small ($-0.009\pm0.111$).
Our setting differs from that of QRKD, so this is not a failed replication. We do not, however, observe consistent gains over classical relational distillation.

The one pattern in favour of \Pqk is a lower spread across seeds (Table~\ref{tab:var}), and it is weaker than it first looks.
On macro-F1, with the validation-selected $\lambda$, its standard deviation is lower than that of \Rbf on \VarBelowRbf{} of \NPairs{} pairs and lower than that of \Scratch on \VarBelowScratch{}. On top-1 the counts are \VarTopBelowRbf{} and \VarTopBelowScratch{}.
Pooled over pairs, the standard deviation is \PooledPqk{} for \Pqk, against \PooledRbf{} for \Rbf and \PooledScratch{} for \Scratch. The \Scratch value includes the recipe-mismatched run on RPS-25/LeNet-5; without that seed it falls to \PooledScratchEx{}, below that of \Pqk.
The advantage over \Rbf is therefore the more consistent one, and the advantage over \Scratch is fragile.
Three further limits apply. A standard deviation from three seeds is itself very uncertain, and a sign test on four of six pairs gives $p=\SignP$. The pattern depends on the calibration: with $\lambda=1$, \Pqk is below \Scratch on three pairs and below \Rbf on five. Without the mismatched seed, the count against \Scratch is \VarBelowScratchEx{} of \NPairs{} on macro-F1 and \VarTopBelowScratchEx{} on top-1.
\Pqk is also not the most stable method. On the five pairs where \Rkd exists, the pooled standard deviations are \PooledScratchFive{} for \Scratch, \PooledRkdFive{} for \Rkd and \PooledPqkFive{} for \Pqk.
After INT8 conversion the pattern weakens further. Pooled over the four exported pairs on PC numerics, \Pqk has \IntPooledPqk{}, against \IntPooledScratch{} for \Scratch and \IntPooledRbf{} for \Rbf. On TrashNet, however, it remains below both on both students under all three sets of kernels.

\subsection{Entanglement and the teacher projection}
\label{sec:results-controls}

\paragraph{Removing the entanglement}
On TrashNet, whose validation split has $379$ images, \Pqk (adaptive $\lambda$) is ahead of its entanglement-free twin on every seed of both students, with validation macro-F1 differences of $+0.049\pm0.065$ and $+0.007\pm0.008$. This is the one consistent sign that the CNOT gates contribute something beyond the sixteen trigonometric coordinates.
On RPS-25, with a $33$-image validation set, the sign reverses ($-0.041$ and $-0.022$). Test scores point the same way, except on TrashNet/MobileNetV3-Small, where the difference vanishes ($-0.002$).
The claim is limited. The entanglement-free readout $(\sin4\theta,\cos4\theta)$ oscillates rapidly over $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ and may simply be a poor classical map, and the generic control designed to settle this was not run.
The class separation of the target kernel did not predict the outcome either. On TrashNet the entanglement-free target is better aligned with the labels ($0.323$ against $0.245$ centered kernel--target alignment on validation), yet students trained on the PQK target rank higher.

\paragraph{A fitted teacher projection}
The fixed random $2048\to8$ projection discards much of the teacher's class structure. On TrashNet validation, the alignment of the teacher kernel with the label kernel falls from $0.720$ for the raw features to $0.363$ for the random angles.
A projection fitted on the training split (LDA+PCA, selected before any student was trained) restores it to $0.825$. It did not help: fitted was better than random in one of eight arm-pairs on validation, the exception being \Pqk on RPS-25/MobileNetV3-Small ($+0.043\pm0.039$).
On TrashNet the fitted target is almost the label kernel on the training batches where the loss is computed. Its alignment is $0.963$ for the RBF target against $0.825$ on validation, and $0.902$ against $0.752$ for the PQK target.
Aligning to such a target asks the student to cluster by class, which cross-entropy already does, and removes the within-class geometry that relational distillation is meant to transfer.
This account does not explain RPS-25, where the fitted PQK target ($0.897$ on training batches against $0.797$ on validation) produced the one positive case, and we did not test it by intervention.

\paragraph{Gradient balance}
With the random target, the kernel term does not dominate training. The ratio of its gradient norm to that of the cross-entropy falls from $0.70$ over the first three epochs to $0.14$ over the last five for the entanglement-free arm ($12$ runs), and from $0.88$ to $0.20$ for \Pqk (two logged runs).
With a fitted target the ratio stays between $0.28$ and $0.74$ on three pairs. On RPS-25/MobileNetV2-0.35 it reaches $3.1$ and $2.1$, so there the kernel term outweighs the label signal.

\subsection{INT8 conversion and on-device behaviour}
\label{sec:results-device}

\begin{table*}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{INT8 models on the Raspberry Pi 4B with the default XNNPACK delegate, four threads, batch size 1: median (p50) and 95th-percentile (p95) invocation time over $500$ calls, mean throughput and the speed-up from one to four threads. Latency and throughput are averages over the methods and seeds of each pair (15--18 models); the thread speed-up is measured on one model per pair. Sizes are in KiB.}
  \label{tab:edge}
  \adjustbox{max width=\textwidth}{\input{generated/tab_edge}}
\end{table*}

All \NIntModels{} exported models pass every check. They are fully integer and per-channel, contain no custom or floating-point operations, and reproduce their fp32 score on reload; their ONNX graphs match the PyTorch logits to within $1.3\times10^{-4}$.
Model size is set by the architecture alone, \MbMnvTwo{}\,MB for MobileNetV2-0.35 and \MbMnvThree{}\,MB for MobileNetV3-Small, so any comparison at matched size holds by construction.
Conversion has no systematic cost. Macro-F1 changes by $\IntDeltaMean\pm\IntDeltaStd$ across the models, and \IntGained{} of them improve.
Conversion does change predictions: INT8 and fp32 disagree on about $7\%$ of test images, in both directions. INT8 rankings between methods are therefore fp32 rankings plus noise.
\Pqk has the most favourable fp32-to-INT8 change on PC numerics ($+0.0080$, up on $9$ of $12$ models, against $-0.0031$ for \Kd). The difference is not significant ($p=\QuantPPc{}$), and the change shrinks to $+0.0025$ with the Pi's default kernels.

On the Raspberry Pi ($160$ runs, none failed; Table~\ref{tab:edge}), the median INT8 latency with four threads is $2.5$\,ms for MobileNetV2-0.35 and $3.2$\,ms for MobileNetV3-Small, about $395$ and $315$ inferences per second.
Latency is a property of the architecture: within a pair, the spread between methods is at most $0.11$\,ms.
The two networks do about the same work ($18.9$ and $18.2$\,M multiply--accumulates). Even so, MobileNetV2 is about $25\%$ faster on four threads and scales better with threads ($1.75$--$1.84\times$ against $1.35$--$1.40\times$).
Against fp32 with the same runtime, INT8 is $1.2$ to $1.4\times$ faster and $2.6$ to $3.1\times$ smaller, and it uses $5.4$--$6.7$\,MB of interpreter memory instead of $8$--$12$\,MB.
XNNPACK is $1.1$ to $1.4\times$ faster than the builtin kernels, but it changes predictions. Per model, it disagrees with the PC on \XnnDisMin{}\% to \XnnDisMax{}\% of test images (\XnnDisMean{}\% on average), against at most \BuiltinDisMax{}\% for the builtin kernels.
These disagreements move per-method macro-F1 by \XnnVsPcMin{} to \XnnVsPcMax{}. They also flip the sign of the \Pqk--\Kd comparison on TrashNet/MobileNetV2-0.35 ($+0.004$ on PC, $-0.004$ with XNNPACK), so INT8 results should be reported with the runtime that produced them.
The SoC reached at most $64.8\,^\circ$C. The firmware did flag under-voltage and throttling in $2$ of the $160$ runs, so the supply is marginal and we attempted no energy measurement.
A live-camera pipeline reaches the webcam's $30$ frames per second with two inference and two OpenCV threads, at $12.8$\,ms of processing per frame. Image preparation ($8.8$\,ms) takes longer than inference ($4.1$\,ms).

The $152$ student runs used \CostKeptHours{} compute-hours, \CostPqkHours{} of them in the \CostPqkRuns{} runs of the \Pqk family. A further \CostDiscardedHours{} hours went into runs discarded after pipeline fixes.
The circuit adds simulation to every training step of a \Pqk run, and none of it remains at deployment, where all methods give models of identical size and speed.
