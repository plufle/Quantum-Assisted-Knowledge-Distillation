\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sorting waste at the point of disposal, for example in a camera-assisted bin that suggests a category for the item presented to it, needs a classifier that runs locally on inexpensive hardware with a small memory footprint and low latency.
The available data are small and imbalanced. TrashNet~\citep{yang2016trashnet} has six material classes, with $96$ to $416$ training images per class in our split.
Compact networks such as MobileNets~\citep{sandler2018mobilenetv2,howard2019searching} and 8-bit integer inference~\citep{jacob2018quantization} reduce the footprint at some cost in accuracy.

Knowledge distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling} recovers part of that accuracy. The compact student is trained to match a larger teacher's outputs as well as the hard labels.
Relational variants transfer the geometry of the teacher's representation instead~\citep{park2019relational,tian2020contrastive}.
Because only the student is deployed, distillation combines naturally with quantization on edge devices~\citep{paparounas2024evaluating}.

Recent work asks whether quantum computation can assist distillation while the deployed model stays classical.
Quantum relational knowledge distillation (QRKD)~\citep{liu2025qrkd} computes quantum-kernel values between features mapped into a Hilbert space and uses them as relational targets. It reports gains over classical relational KD, with quantum computation needed only in training.
Other work distills classical teachers into quantum students~\citep{li2024hybrid,hasan2023bridging} or uses a quantum model as a soft-label teacher~\citep{naqvi2026structured}.
We ask a narrower question: does the quantum component contribute anything that a classical kernel in the same place would not?

For kernels, the answer is not obvious.
Quantum kernel values can concentrate until they no longer distinguish inputs~\citep{thanasilp2024exponential}. Numerical studies find no advantage for fidelity kernels on classical data~\citep{slattery2023numerical}, and bandwidth-tuned quantum kernels can resemble classical ones~\citep{florezablan2025similarity}.
The projected quantum kernel (PQK) of Huang et al.~\citep{huang2021power} is a clean candidate to test. At small scale it can be simulated classically, and a classical kernel acting on the same inputs can replace it one-for-one.
Two further points shape the comparison. Effects on small datasets are comparable to seed-to-seed noise, so conclusions need several seeds and paired comparisons. The end point is the deployed INT8 model, and its results depend on the kernels that execute it.

We therefore compare quantum-assisted and classical distillation under one pipeline.
TrashNet is the primary dataset. RPS-25, a 25\% training subset of Rock--Paper--Scissors~\citep{moroney2019rps}, serves as a low-data confirmation.
A frozen ResNet-50 teacher~\citep{he2016deep} distills into three students of 0.03\,M to 0.93\,M parameters. We compare five methods: hard-label training (\Scratch), logit KD (\Kd), relational KD (\Rkd), classical RBF-kernel alignment (\Rbf, the matched twin) and PQK (\Pqk).
Each cell has \NSeeds{} seeds, and every exported model is a plain INT8 network with no quantum operation.

Our findings are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \Pqk is not distinguishable from \Kd. It is ahead on the mean in \KdAheadFp{} of \NPairs{} pairs and behind on both TrashNet MobileNets (by \KdBehindTnA{} and \KdBehindTnB{} macro-F1). The smallest uncorrected paired $p$-value is \MinPKd{}.
  \item At its pre-specified kernel scale, \Pqk beats its RBF twin in \GtwoFixedWins{} of \NPairs{} pairs. With a median-heuristic scale adopted after these results were seen, it does so in \GtwoAdaptWins{} of \GtwoAdaptPairs{}, and the one nominally significant difference favours \MinPRbfAdaptFavours.
  \item \Pqk tends to have lower seed-to-seed variance than \Rbf and, less consistently, than \Scratch. The difference is not significant at three seeds.
  \item All \NIntModels{} exported MobileNet students are fully integer INT8 models. INT8 conversion changes macro-F1 by $\IntDeltaMean$ on average, and the size of each method's INT8 effect depends on the runtime kernels.
\end{itemize}
All comparisons are exploratory. Cells have three seeds and the test splits informed earlier design choices, so we select on validation and treat test scores as descriptive.

We make three contributions.
(i)~A matched-control comparison of quantum-assisted and classical distillation for lightweight waste classification, with a classical twin for the quantum kernel and paired per-seed reporting. In it, \Pqk is ahead of logit KD in \KdAheadFp{} of \NPairs{} pairs and ahead of its twin in \GtwoFixedWins{} of \NPairs{} at its pre-specified scale.
(ii)~Two ablations that probe where a quantum-kernel effect could come from. Removing the circuit's entanglement lowers TrashNet validation macro-F1 on every seed but raises it on RPS-25, and a teacher projection fitted to the labels beats the random one in only one of eight arm-pairs.
(iii)~Deployment evidence on a Raspberry Pi~4B. The INT8 students run in \LatMnvTwo{} and \LatMnvThree{}\,ms, and the device's default kernels change \XnnDisMin{}\% to \XnnDisMax{}\% of a model's predictions relative to the PC.
