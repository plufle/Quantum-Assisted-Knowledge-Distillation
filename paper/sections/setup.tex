\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:setup-data}

TrashNet~\citep{yang2016trashnet} has \(2{,}527\) photographs in six classes (cardboard, glass, metal, paper, plastic, trash) and no official split.
We split it once, stratified $70/15/15$ with seed $0$ ($1{,}768$/$379$/$380$ images), and never re-split it.
The classes are imbalanced ($96$ to $416$ training images per class). Macro-F1 is therefore the primary metric, and training batches use a class-balanced sampler.
RPS-25 is a $25\%$ subset of the Rock--Paper--Scissors training images~\citep{moroney2019rps} ($630$ images, $210$ per class). We kept it deliberately low-data so that distillation has room to help.
It keeps the official validation ($33$ images) and test ($372$ images) splits, and top-1 accuracy is its primary metric. We treat it as a low-data confirmation, not as a second application.
All images are resized to $128{\times}128$ and normalized with ImageNet statistics.
Training uses random resized crops, RandAugment~\citep{cubuk2020randaugment} and horizontal flips, identically for every method. The crop's minimum scale is $0.85$ on TrashNet and $0.7$ on RPS-25, and RandAugment applies one operation at magnitude $5$ on TrashNet and two at magnitude $9$ on RPS-25.

\subsection{Models and training}
\label{sec:setup-training}

The teacher is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50~\citep{he2016deep}, fine-tuned once per dataset (AdamW, learning rate $10^{-4}$, at most $20$ epochs, patience $5$) and then frozen in evaluation mode. Its test macro-F1 is \TeacherTn{} on TrashNet and \TeacherRps{} on RPS-25.
The students are trained from random initialization. They are MobileNetV2 with width multiplier $0.35$ (0.40\,M parameters), MobileNetV3-Small (0.93\,M) and a LeNet-5 variant with global average pooling before the classifier (0.03\,M). Only the two MobileNets, the edge-relevant students, are exported.

All methods on a pair share the data, the optimizer (AdamW, learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine schedule), the batch size ($128$), the epoch budget and the early-stopping patience.
The budget is $60$ epochs with patience $10$ for MobileNetV2-0.35, $40$ and $8$ for MobileNetV3-Small, and $60$ and $12$ for LeNet-5.
The best validation checkpoint is restored. On TrashNet it is selected by validation macro-F1. On RPS-25, whose $33$-image validation split makes F1 too erratic, it is selected by validation loss, meaning each method's own objective with the kernel term at $\gamma_{\max}$.
On RPS-25, and for MobileNetV3-Small on TrashNet, the running BatchNorm statistics did not stabilize. There, BatchNorm uses per-batch statistics during training, and a recalibration pass on clean training images precedes every evaluation.
\Kd uses $\alpha=\beta=0.5$ with $\tau=3$ on TrashNet and $\tau=1$ on RPS-25. Both values were screened on MobileNetV2-0.35 and reused for the other students.
\Rkd uses distance and angle weights $5$ and $10$ ($1$ and $2$ for LeNet-5 on TrashNet). The kernel methods use $\tau=0.5$ and the $\gamma$ ramp of Section~\ref{sec:kernel-alignment}.
Two cells depart from the shared recipe, both on RPS-25/LeNet-5. One \Scratch seed used a $40$-epoch budget with patience $8$, and the \Kd cell mixes $\tau=0.5$ (seed~0) and $\tau=1$ (seeds~1 and~2). We did not re-run them; Section~\ref{sec:results} states where they matter.
Each cell is trained with seeds $0$, $1$ and $2$, which set the student initialization, the data order and the random teacher projection.

\subsection{Selection, reporting and the status of the results}
\label{sec:setup-protocol}

Every number is the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation ($n{-}1$) over three seeds.
Comparisons are paired by seed. We report the mean paired difference, its standard deviation and the number of seeds on which the first method is ahead. Two-sided paired $t$-tests are descriptive and uncorrected for multiple comparisons.
For the follow-up arms, a shared seed gives identical student initialization and data order, because the first-batch cross-entropy and KL gradients are identical across the arms. Stored logs confirm this for \texttt{noent\_control}, \texttt{pqk\_fit} and \texttt{rbf\_fit} in every cell, and for two \texttt{pqk\_adaptive} runs. For the earlier grid, pairing means a shared seed value only.

The results are exploratory, for three reasons.
First, the student recipes, the KD temperature and the RKD weights were screened on seed~0, and some of these choices were judged by test scores.
Second, the kernel methods' $\tau=0.5$ was inherited from a KD screen on LeNet-5 with RPS-25 and was not re-tuned, whereas the \Kd temperature was screened. Comparisons between a kernel method and \Kd are therefore not matched on that parameter and may disadvantage the kernel methods.
Third, the scale $\lambda$ of \Pqk was chosen after results were seen. The pre-specified $\lambda=1$ gave the \Pqk results of the first full grid. The adaptive $\lambda$ was adopted afterwards, per pair, by the higher three-seed \emph{validation} macro-F1 of the two variants.
For this choice and for the follow-up analyses, validation decides and test scores are descriptive. The headline comparisons show both $\lambda$ variants.
A confirmatory claim would need a frozen protocol, selection on validation alone and fresh data.

\subsection{INT8 export and on-device benchmark}
\label{sec:setup-int8}

Each MobileNet student is stripped of its projection and kernel modules and converted to ONNX (opset 17, static batch size 1). It is then converted to a fully integer TFLite model with per-channel weight quantization, calibrated on $256$ unaugmented training images.
Every model passes four checks: purity (no projection, quantum or custom operations), a fully integer, per-channel graph, ONNX--PyTorch logit parity, and an fp32 reload that reproduces the recorded test score.
INT8 test scores are measured with the LiteRT interpreter on an x86 PC (``PC'' below).
The $\NIntModels{}$ exported models cover two students, two datasets, five methods and three seeds, plus the second $\lambda$ variant of \Pqk on two pairs. For TrashNet/MobileNetV2-0.35 the exported \Pqk uses $\lambda=1$, whereas the final validation rule prefers adaptive $\lambda$.

The INT8 models are benchmarked on a Raspberry Pi~4 Model~B (Cortex-A72, four cores, 4\,GB, Ubuntu 20.04, 600--1800\,MHz) with LiteRT (\texttt{ai-edge-litert} 2.1.0).
Each model runs in a fresh process with $50$ warm-up and $500$ timed invocations at batch size~1, starting below $55\,^\circ$C. We log latency percentiles, CPU use, memory, SoC temperature and clock, and the firmware's throttling flags.
The suite runs every model with the default XNNPACK delegate and with the builtin kernels. It adds a thread sweep from one to four threads, fp32 references, on-device test scores compared with the PC's predictions, and a live USB-camera pipeline ($640{\times}480$).
Energy, the Jetson Orin Nano and the ESP32-S3 were not measured.

All training ran on a single GPU, often with several runs sharing it, so we report compute-hours rather than per-epoch times.
The $152$ student runs include the grid, the follow-up arms, the screens, and seven runs of two wider MobileNetV2 variants that were stopped as capacity probes and are not reported.
