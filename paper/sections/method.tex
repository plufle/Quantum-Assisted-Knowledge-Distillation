\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pipeline}
  \caption{Training-time pipeline. Penultimate features of the frozen teacher and of the student are projected to eight angles and compared through the Gram matrices of one kernel (the PQK circuit or an RBF kernel). Everything inside the dashed region is discarded at export; only the student CNN is deployed.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

Let $x$ be an image with label $y$. We write $z_t(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2048}$ and $z_s(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ for the penultimate features of the teacher and the student, and $\ell_t,\ell_s$ for their logits.
All losses are computed on batches of up to $B=128$ images. The last batch of an epoch is smaller, and the $33$-image RPS-25 validation split forms a single batch.

\subsection{Baseline objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

\Scratch minimizes the cross-entropy $\mathrm{CE}(\ell_s,y)$.
\Kd is Hinton distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  L_{\mathrm{KD}} ={}& \alpha\,\mathrm{CE}(\ell_s,y)\\
  &+ \beta\,\tau^{2}\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_t/\tau)\,\Vert\,\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_s/\tau)\right),
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=\beta=0.5$. The temperature $\tau$ was screened on some pairs and reused on others (Section~\ref{sec:setup-training}).
\Rkd~\citep{park2019relational} adds distance-wise and angle-wise relational terms on the penultimate features,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  L_{\mathrm{RKD}} ={}& \mathrm{CE}(\ell_s,y) + w_d\,\mathrm{SmoothL1}\big(\psi_D(z_s),\psi_D(z_t)\big)\\
  &+ w_a\,\mathrm{SmoothL1}\big(\psi_A(z_s),\psi_A(z_t)\big),
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_D$ collects the pairwise distances in a batch, normalized by their mean, and $\psi_A$ the cosines of the angles formed by triplets.
Both potentials are dimension-free, so a 2048-dimensional teacher can be compared directly with a student of any width.

\subsection{Kernel alignment}
\label{sec:kernel-alignment}

The two kernel methods, \Pqk and its twin \Rbf, add a kernel-alignment term to the logit objective:
\begin{equation}
  L = \alpha\,\mathrm{CE}(\ell_s,y) + \beta\,\tau^{2}\,\mathrm{KL}(\cdot) + \gamma_e\,L_{\mathrm{kernel}},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{kernel}} = \big\Vert \tilde K_t - \tilde K_s \big\Vert_F^{2},\qquad
  \tilde K = \frac{K}{\operatorname{tr}K}.
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha=\beta=0.5$, and all kernel methods share the temperature $\tau=0.5$.
The weight $\gamma_e=\gamma_{\max}\min\!\big(1,(e{+}1)/10\big)$ rises linearly over the first ten epochs $e$ to $\gamma_{\max}=1$.
Validation is always scored at $\gamma_{\max}$, so the ramp cannot change which checkpoint is selected.
$K_t$ and $K_s$ are the $B\times B$ Gram matrices of the teacher and the student, built from eight angles per image:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_s = \pi\,\sigma(W_s z_s + b_s),\quad
  \theta_t = \pi\,\sigma(W_t z_t + b_t),\quad \theta\in[0,\pi]^{8}.
\end{equation}
The student-side projection $(W_s,b_s)$ is trained jointly with the student, and gradients reach the student through the kernel.
The teacher-side projection $(W_t,b_t)$ is drawn at default initialization at the start of each run, then frozen and evaluated without gradients.
Freezing it is necessary. If both projections were trainable, collapsing both to constant maps would set every Gram entry to one and make the two normalized matrices identical, so $L_{\mathrm{kernel}}$ would reach zero without the student learning anything.
Our first implementation had this flaw: in the first \Pqk run we inspected (RPS-25, LeNet-5, seed~0), the mean off-diagonal kernel value was \CollapseStudent{} for the student and \CollapseTeacher{} for the teacher\extra{; Appendix~\ref{app:pitfalls} gives the details}.
We now log the mean off-diagonal kernel value in every epoch and treat values below $0.05$ or above $0.95$ as degenerate.

\subsection{The projected quantum kernel}
\label{sec:pqk}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pqk_circuit}
  \caption{The fixed feature map behind \Pqk (shown for qubits $0$, $1$, $2$ and $7$). Each of the eight angles drives an $R_Y$ rotation, followed by an open chain of CNOT gates; the pair is repeated $4$ times with the same angles. Only single-qubit expectation values are read out. Nothing in the circuit is trainable.}
  \label{fig:circuit}
\end{figure*}

The quantum kernel is the projected quantum kernel of Huang et al.~\citep{huang2021power}.
The circuit (Figure~\ref{fig:circuit}) acts on $8$ qubits that start in $|0\rangle^{\otimes 8}$. It applies $R_Y(\theta_q)$ to every qubit $q$, then a CNOT from qubit $q$ to $q{+}1$ for $q=0,\dots,6$, and repeats this pair of layers $4$ times.
The CNOTs form an open chain; no gate closes it into a ring.
For each input we measure the Bloch vector of every qubit, $r(\theta)=(\langle X_q\rangle,\langle Y_q\rangle,\langle Z_q\rangle)_{q=0}^{7}$.
$R_Y$ and CNOT are real matrices, so the state stays real and $\langle Y_q\rangle\equiv0$. Only $16$ of the $24$ coordinates are therefore active.
The kernel compares inputs through their single-qubit reduced states $\rho_q=\tfrac12(I+r_q\cdot\sigma)$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  K(\theta,\theta') &= \exp\!\Big(-\lambda\sum_{q}\big\Vert\rho_q(\theta)-\rho_q(\theta')\big\Vert_F^{2}\Big)\\
  &= \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,\big\Vert r(\theta)-r(\theta')\big\Vert^{2}\Big),
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:pqk}
\end{equation}
where the second form follows from $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_a\sigma_b)=2\delta_{ab}$.
Only single-qubit expectation values are read out, and at eight qubits the circuit is cheap to simulate exactly. We use PennyLane~\citep{bergholm2018pennylane} with its state-vector \texttt{lightning.qubit} backend and adjoint differentiation.

We set the scale $\lambda$ in two ways.
\Pqk uses the pre-specified $\lambda=1$.
The variant \texttt{pqk\_adaptive}, adopted after the first results (Section~\ref{sec:setup-protocol}), sets $\lambda$ in every batch so that $K=\exp\!\big(-d^{2}/(2m)\big)$, where $d^{2}=\tfrac12\Vert\Delta r\Vert^{2}$ and $m$ is the median off-diagonal $d^{2}$ of the batch.
The Gram matrix is then the median-heuristic RBF kernel on the measured features $r(\theta)$.

\subsection{Matched classical controls}
\label{sec:controls}

\Rbf is the same pipeline with an RBF kernel, $K=\exp\!\big(-\Vert\theta-\theta'\Vert^{2}/(2m)\big)$, applied to the same eight angles with the same median heuristic.
The quantum branch with adaptive $\lambda$ and \Rbf thus differ only in the map $\theta\mapsto r(\theta)$ that precedes an identically calibrated RBF kernel.
Two further arms isolate where an effect of that map could come from.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{noent\_control} deletes the CNOT gates. Each qubit then sees $R_Y(\theta_q)^{4}=R_Y(4\theta_q)$, so $r=(\sin4\theta_q,0,\cos4\theta_q)$ exactly (checked against the simulator to $6\times10^{-8}$). The map keeps the $16$ active coordinates, their range, the absence of trainable parameters and the adaptive RBF kernel, and differs from \texttt{pqk\_adaptive} only by entanglement.
  \item \texttt{pqk\_fit} and \texttt{rbf\_fit} replace the random teacher-side projection with a map fitted once on the training split and then frozen. It consists of the $C{-}1$ shrinkage-LDA directions, completed to eight outputs with the leading principal components of the residual and standardized on the training set.
\end{itemize}
A third control, random Fourier features drawn from the circuit's own frequency spectrum, was implemented but not run\extra{ (Section~\ref{sec:limitations})}.
All kernel arms use eight angles, the loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} and the same frozen teacher, and every circuit has $8$ qubits and depth $4$.

\subsection{What is trained and what is deployed}
\label{sec:deployed}

Only the student CNN is exported.
The projection heads, the circuit and the kernels exist only inside the training loop, and a purity check fails if any of them appears in the exported graph (Section~\ref{sec:setup-int8}).
For every method, including \Pqk, the deployed model is an ordinary convolutional network; the quantum kernel affects it only through the weights it learned.
