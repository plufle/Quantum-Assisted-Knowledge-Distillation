Knowledge distillation (KD) suits camera-assisted waste sorting on low-power hardware: a large teacher guides a compact student, and only the student is deployed.
A recent proposal adds a quantum kernel to the distillation loss. Quantum computation is then needed only during training, and the deployed network stays classical.
Whether such a kernel helps beyond kernel alignment itself requires matched classical controls.
We test this on TrashNet, with RPS-25 as a low-data confirmation. The setup uses a ResNet-50 teacher, three students (LeNet-5, MobileNetV2-0.35, MobileNetV3-Small) and one shared training pipeline.
We compare hard-label training, logit KD, relational KD, a classical RBF-kernel twin and a projected quantum kernel (PQK). Each cell has three seeds (\NGridRuns{} grid runs; \NStudentRuns{} student runs including controls, screens and capacity probes).
PQK is not distinguishable from logit KD. It is ahead on the mean in \KdAheadFp{} of \NPairs{} student--dataset pairs and behind on both TrashNet MobileNets, and no paired difference is significant (smallest uncorrected $p=\MinPKd$).
At its pre-specified kernel scale, PQK beats its RBF twin in only \GtwoFixedWins{} of \NPairs{} pairs.
Its seed-to-seed variance tends to be lower than that of the RBF twin and, less consistently, of hard-label training. This is not significant at three seeds and does not hold on every pair.
All \NIntModels{} exported MobileNet students convert to fully integer INT8 models of about \MbMnvTwo{} and \MbMnvThree{}~MB. On a Raspberry Pi~4B they run in \LatMnvTwo{} and \LatMnvThree{}~ms per inference (median, four threads).
INT8 conversion changes macro-F1 by $\IntDeltaMean$ on average (std.\ \IntDeltaStd{} across models), and the sign of per-method effects depends on which INT8 kernels run.
The quantum kernel leaves no trace in the deployed model, but our evidence does not show an accuracy benefit over classical alternatives.
All results are exploratory.
